Broadband Access and Low Opportunity Areas Full Project Description

Opportunity Mapping: Broadband Access explores broadband availability of varying download speeds across California that intersect with low opportunity areas.

This project aims to identify and address disparities in broadband access to support equitable opportunities for all communities.

Mapping Broadband Opportunity in California

Mapping Broadband Opportunity in California.

Welcome to the Broadband Access Opportunity Mapping tool. This interactive map attempts to answer a critical question:

Which “low opportunity” geographic areas with limited access to high-speed broadband should be prioritized to bridge the digital divide?

Our organization undertook this mapping effort to provide data-driven insights and an evidence-based framework for understanding the neighborhood characteristics that matter when expanding broadband access. Drawing on existing research, we developed maps designed to inform policy discussions and community engagement around where and how broadband infrastructure can have the greatest impact.

It is important to note, however, that these maps address only one dimension of California’s broadband challenge—access and opportunity patterns. Other critical considerations, such as affordability, digital literacy, and computer access, require additional analysis and dedicated stakeholder engagement to ensure broadband investments are both equitable and effective.

Low Opportunity

Low Opportunity areas are parts of the state with characteristics that research has shown to be strongly associated with poorer economic, educational, and health outcomes for low-income families—especially long-term outcomes for children—compared to other neighborhoods in the same region

Broadband Speed Definition

Broadband speeds are defined based on Federal Communications Commission (FCC) defined "maximum advertised download speed" offered to end users. This analysis brackets download speeds in two categories "Under 10 Mbps" and "10 Mbps to 25 Mbps". We also determined how many households have access to Fiber broadband.

Broadband Access Codes

Broadband access is categorized based on the following FCC broadband access codes:

  • 10: Copper wire-based connections.
  • 40: Coaxial cable-based or hybrid fiber- coaxial connections.
  • 50: Optical Fiber-based connections.
